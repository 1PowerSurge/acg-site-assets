Why a Printed Resume Package Looks More Professional Than a Home Printout

July 11, 2025

Most job seekers submit their resumes digitally, which means showing up to an interview with a professionally printed package already sets you apart. But there is a significant difference between running your resume through a home inkjet printer and having it printed on proper resume stock by a professional service. That difference is immediately felt the moment someone picks it up — the weight of the paper, the sharpness of the text, the absence of streaking or smearing. These physical qualities communicate care and preparation before a single sentence is read.

What Professional Printing Actually Changes

Home inkjet and laser printers produce acceptable results for drafts and internal documents, but they have real limitations for professional-facing print. Inkjet ink can smear when wet and tends to look slightly softer than commercial digital print. Laser toner can show a slight sheen or texture on quality paper stocks. Commercial digital printing produces crisp, consistent results with sharp text edges and stable color that does not vary from the first copy to the last.

The paper makes an equal difference. Standard printer paper is typically 20lb bond — functional but thin. Professional resume printing uses 24lb or 32lb resume paper, which has more body and opacity. Linen stock, with its subtle texture, is a step further — a common choice for legal, executive, and creative industry candidates. The weight and feel of the paper communicate seriousness in a way that standard printer output cannot.

What to Include in a Complete Resume Package

A professional resume package typically includes four components: the resume, a cover letter, a reference sheet, and a business card. Having all four printed consistently on matching paper stock creates a cohesive presentation. When you hand this to an interviewer or recruiter, it reads as organized and deliberate. The business card is often unexpected — easy to hand across a table and something the interviewer can keep beyond the stack of documents.

File Prep for Professional Resume Printing

Send your resume files as PDF with all fonts embedded. This prevents font substitution, which can change your carefully set line breaks and column alignments. Use a minimum of 0.5 inch margins on all sides — tight margins that print fine at home can get clipped on a commercial press. If your resume uses color, keep it minimal: a single accent color in a header or rule line is professional; heavy color backgrounds do not reproduce consistently on resume stock.

For black and white resumes, confirm your grays are set as K (black) only rather than a CMYK mix. This produces cleaner grays and avoids any registration shift that makes a gray tone look slightly warm or cool depending on the press.

When It Is Worth Having It Done Professionally

For a final-round interview, a creative portfolio review, or any situation where you are one of a small number of candidates in a room — a professional package is worth it. The cost to print a small set of resume packages is modest, and the impression it leaves is not.
