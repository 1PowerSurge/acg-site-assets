How to Print a Resume Package That Looks More Professional Than a Standard Home Printout

July 11, 2025

Resume printing may sound simple, but a polished resume package is more than just black text on white paper. Clients who ask for printed resumes usually care about presentation, and that means the details matter. A resume that feels crisp, balanced, and intentional can support the applicant’s confidence before a conversation even starts.

For a home business, resume packages can be a practical short-run service because they usually involve very low quantity, fast turnaround, and a client who values clean presentation. The key is to treat the package like a coordinated set, not a stack of separate sheets.

Understand what belongs in the package

Some clients only need resumes. Others need a matching cover letter, references, or a simple portfolio page. Clarify that at the start. It changes the quantity, paper planning, and finishing.

A basic package might include:

• resume

• cover letter

• references page

A more complete package might also include:

• a light personal branding header

• a matching folder or presentation sleeve

• a simple one-page work sample or certifications list

The goal is not to overpackage the job. The goal is to help the client look organized and serious.

Use the right paper

Paper choice changes the perceived quality immediately. Standard copy paper can work for internal drafts, but when a client is paying for final presentation, stepping up the sheet makes sense. A smoother, heavier text stock often looks cleaner and handles better. Bright white can create a crisp appearance, while softer white can feel more classic.

What matters most is that the paper looks intentional and prints cleanly. If the sheet is too thin, the package feels weak. If it is too heavy for the printer or causes feed issues, production becomes frustrating for no good reason.

For many short-run resume jobs, a quality text stock gives enough upgrade to feel professional without becoming excessive.

Make the documents feel connected

One of the easiest ways to improve a resume package is to make the pages feel related. That does not mean turning the resume into a flashy marketing piece. It means using consistent margins, type choices, header treatment, and spacing across the set.

A matching header with the client’s name and contact details can quietly unify the resume, cover letter, and references page. Consistent typography matters too. A package feels more polished when the files clearly belong together.

For small-batch work, this is valuable because the upgrade comes from coordination, not from expensive finishing.

Watch the print details

Resume work is unforgiving because it is mostly text. That means flaws are easier to see. Low toner density, grayish black text, misalignment, crooked cutting, or wrinkled sheets can make the package feel rushed.

Before delivering:

• check margins

• check line spacing

• confirm page order

• verify names, numbers, email, and dates

• print a proof to review how the text weight looks

• make sure the printer is not introducing streaks or shadowing

Because the content is usually formal, production mistakes stand out more than they might on a colorful flyer.

Keep the design professional

A resume package should look refined, not overdecorated. Avoid adding unnecessary color blocks, multiple font families, busy icons, or trendy design elements that distract from the person’s qualifications. A little structure is good. Noise is not.

Most strong resume packages use:

• clean typography

• balanced white space

• modest emphasis

• consistent section hierarchy

• simple headings

That approach works for most industries and keeps the focus where it belongs.

Offer useful finish options without overcomplicating

Even in a small home setup, a resume package can feel more complete when the presentation is considered. That might mean crisp corner alignment, a subtle presentation folder, or a clean clip instead of a loose stack. Some clients only need a neat set of pages. Others may appreciate a simple upgrade if they are attending a fair, interview, or formal meeting.

The main thing is not to oversell extras that do not improve the result. Presentation should support the content, not distract from it.

Common mistakes in resume packages

Problems often come from:

• printing on ordinary paper when the client expects a finished look

• mixing inconsistent document styles

• allowing widows, orphans, or awkward page breaks

• using weak black output that looks gray

• failing to review contact details one last time

• adding unnecessary design touches that reduce credibility

These mistakes are small individually, but together they make the package feel less serious.

Why this is a useful short-run service

Resume packages are manageable because the order size is small, the materials are straightforward, and the result feels valuable to the client. This kind of project also teaches discipline. It forces attention to spacing, consistency, and print cleanliness. Those habits transfer well to many other client jobs.

And while resume orders are typically small, they often lead to related requests such as business cards, certificates, small portfolios, brochures, or event materials.

Closing thought

A strong resume package does not need flashy tricks. It needs clean documents, the right paper, and a presentation that feels deliberate. As client work expands into larger or more coordinated print needs, production consistency and finishing become more important.
