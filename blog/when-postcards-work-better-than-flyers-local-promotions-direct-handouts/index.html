When Postcards Work Better Than Flyers for Local Promotions and Direct Handouts

December 12, 2025

When most small businesses think about printed promotions, they default to flyers. Flyers are familiar, but they also have a well-known fate — folded in half, shoved in a pocket, and eventually thrown away. A postcard is a different object entirely. It is rigid, it fits cleanly in a wallet or on a desk, and it reads as a self-contained piece of communication rather than advertising. For many local promotions and direct handouts, a postcard delivers better results per dollar than a standard flyer.

Why Postcards Perform Better in Certain Situations

Because a postcard is printed on card stock rather than paper, it holds its shape after being carried, stored, or handled repeatedly. A postcard left on a counter or tucked into a stack of papers is still readable days or weeks later. This durability gives your message a longer lifespan than a paper flyer that survives one trip home. Postcards are also double-sided by nature — the front carries your visual and headline, and the back carries the specific information: the offer, contact details, and call to action. This structure forces cleaner design decisions and tends to produce better-performing pieces than a single-surface flyer trying to hold everything at once.

The Right Use Cases for Postcards

Appointment reminders are one of the strongest use cases for postcards in a small service business. A postcard reminder handed to a client at the end of a visit is more likely to make it to their refrigerator or desk than an email notification that disappears into a full inbox. Local service promotions — lawn care, cleaning, pet grooming, tutoring — work well because recipients can hold onto the card until they are ready to act. Event invitations and market handouts also benefit from the postcard format, which feels more like something to keep than something to discard.

Standard Sizes and Design Structure

The three most common postcard sizes are 4 x 6, 5 x 7, and 6 x 9. The 4 x 6 is the most economical and pocket-friendly. The 5 x 7 gives more design room while still being easy to carry. The 6 x 9 is useful when you have a more complex offer to communicate on the back. Design the front for impact: a strong visual, a bold headline, and nothing else. The back should lead with the offer or key information, followed by clear contact details and a single call to action. One clear message wins.

Print Specifications

Print postcards on 14pt or 16pt coated card stock. The coating protects the surface and keeps the print from scuffing during handling. Set up your file with 0.125 inch bleed on all sides and keep all text and design elements at least 0.25 inches from the trim edge. Resolution should be 300dpi at the final print size in CMYK color mode. If your postcard will be mailed, check USPS postcard dimension requirements — standard postcard mailing rates apply to pieces between 3.5 x 5 and 4.25 x 6 inches, and anything outside those dimensions mails at letter rate.
