Beginner Tips for Ordering Window Decals

March 17, 2026

Window decals are one of the most effective and affordable ways for a small business to brand its physical space — but they are also a product where first-time buyers regularly make expensive mistakes. The main tripping points are choosing the wrong vinyl type, confusing inside versus outside application, and submitting a file that does not translate well to cut or printed vinyl. Getting these details right before you order means the decal looks exactly as intended once it is installed.

Understanding the Three Main Vinyl Types

Clear vinyl is printed on transparent film and applied to the inside of a window, facing outward so it is viewed from the street. Because the film is clear, any unprinted area shows the window and interior behind it — this works well for logos, hours, and simple graphics where the design stands on its own. White vinyl is opaque and typically applied to the outside of the glass, producing more vivid color and higher contrast because there is no background showing through. Confirm that your printer uses UV-resistant ink and laminate for any white vinyl applied outdoors. Perforated vinyl, sometimes called see-through vinyl, uses a 50/50 pattern of printed surface and open holes to allow partial visibility from inside while displaying a full graphic from outside. It is best for large storefront windows or vehicle rear windows, and not ideal for fine text or small logos where the dot pattern reduces readability.

Inside vs. Outside Application: The Design Flip

Whether your decal is applied to the inside or outside of the glass changes how the artwork needs to be prepared. A decal applied to the inside and viewed from outside must be printed in reverse — mirrored — so it reads correctly from the street. Printers typically handle this flip automatically for inside-applied graphics, but confirm whether they expect a mirrored file from you or will flip it on their end. Ordering an outside-applied decal when you need an inside-applied one is one of the most common first-time errors and requires a full reprint to correct.

Measuring and File Preparation

Measure the actual glass area — not the window frame. A decal sized to the full opening including the frame will not fit cleanly on the glass. Account for any trim, mullions, or hardware that interrupts the glass surface. For the file, a PNG with a transparent background is the right choice for logo or text decals where the cut follows the shape of the artwork. For full-bleed rectangular window graphics, PDF with vector art is the cleanest option. Set your resolution to 300dpi at the final print size, and use CMYK color mode. Low-resolution logo files — pulled from a website or email signature — are a frequent source of problems. If your logo is a small JPEG or 72dpi PNG, request a vector version before placing the order.

Common Mistakes That Lead to Reprints

Beyond the application-side error and low-resolution files, first-time buyers often forget to measure around window frames, order clear vinyl for a design that needs an opaque background to read correctly, or submit an RGB file that shifts noticeably when converted to CMYK. Confirming material type, application side, dimensions, and file specs with your printer before the order goes into production is worth the extra five minutes.
