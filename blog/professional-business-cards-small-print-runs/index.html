How to Get Professional Business Cards from a Small Print Run

January 24, 2025

Ordering business cards in small quantities — say, 25 to 100 — is one of the most common print jobs for home-based businesses and freelancers. The challenge is that small runs have less room to hide mistakes. A low-res logo or a missing bleed shows up just as clearly on 50 cards as it would on 5,000. Getting it right means understanding a handful of specs that professional printers use every day.

Paper Weight Makes the First Impression

The feel of a business card communicates something before anyone reads a single word. Use at minimum 14pt card stock — this is the standard commercial weight with a solid, substantial feel that does not bend or flop when handed over. If you want to step up, 16pt gives an even thicker result. Finish is the next decision. Matte coating is clean and easy to write on — useful if clients need to jot a note on the back. Gloss gives vivid, high-contrast color and a polished look. Soft touch (sometimes called velvet laminate) is a matte finish with a tactile quality that feels premium and is increasingly popular. Choose a finish that matches your brand rather than the default option.

File Prep: What Printers Actually Need

The standard business card size is 3.5 x 2 inches, but your file should be set up slightly larger to account for bleed. Bleed means extending your background color or design 0.125 inches beyond all four edges of the finished size, giving the cutter room to trim without leaving a thin white line. Keep any text or important design elements at least 0.125 inches inside the trim line — this is your safe zone.

Export your file as a PDF at 300dpi. Critically, your color mode must be CMYK — not RGB. RGB is for screens. When a printer converts an RGB file, colors can shift noticeably, especially deep blues and vibrant oranges. Set your document to CMYK from the beginning, or convert before export and check the color preview carefully.

Where Most Small-Run Jobs Go Wrong

The three most common problems are an RGB color file, missing or insufficient bleed, and low-resolution images or logos. If your logo was pulled from a website, it is almost certainly 72dpi — designed for screens, not print. You need either the original high-res file or a vector version (AI, EPS, or SVG) that scales without quality loss. A pixelated logo on a business card is harder to recover from than no card at all.

Also watch for thin fonts at small sizes. A light-weight typeface below 7pt may not hold cleanly in print, especially on certain finishes. Bump your minimum font size up and check a proof before committing to your full quantity.

Getting It to Print

For short runs, digital printing is the right method — no plate setup costs and clean results at quantities as low as 25. Review a digital proof before approving, and if you are printing for a client, get their sign-off on the proof too. Catching a typo before production costs nothing. Catching it after 100 cards are printed costs the whole job.
